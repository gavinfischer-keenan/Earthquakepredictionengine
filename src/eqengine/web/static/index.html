💡 Observatory Guide — How to read live 4-channel seismograms (Normal vs Earthquake) ▼ Details
ℹ️ WHAT IT MEASURES

EHZ: High-sensitivity vertical geophone (ground velocity in counts).
ENZ/ENN/ENE: Triaxial strong-motion accelerometers (vertical, N-S, E-W in m/s²).

🟢 WHAT IS NORMAL (AMBIENT)

Quiet background is a thin line (±20–150 counts, <0.0003 m/s²). Footsteps/stairs cause isolated sharp micro-spikes (<0.4s). Passing trucks cause narrow 8–15 Hz wave trains lasting 5–10s.

⚡ WHAT AN EARTHQUAKE LOOKS LIKE

Begins with a sharp, high-frequency P-wave arrival on EHZ/ENZ, followed seconds later by powerful rolling horizontal S-waves on ENN/ENE (3–5x larger amplitude) that slowly decay over tens of seconds.

💡 Observatory Guide — Understanding the HD Frequency Waterfall (0–50 Hz) ▼ Details
ℹ️ WHAT IT MEASURES

Real-time 128-bin FFT power spectrum from 0 Hz (bottom) to 50 Hz (top) scrolling over time. Deep Navy = quiet; Yellow/Red = intense seismic energy.

🟢 WHAT IS NORMAL (AMBIENT)

Quiet ambient background is solid deep navy. Heavy diesel trucks create narrow streaks around 8–14 Hz. Greek Theatre concerts appear as low-bass bands around 25–45 Hz. Cal Stadium crowds create 2–8 Hz acoustic hum.

⚡ WHAT AN EARTHQUAKE LOOKS LIKE

A sudden, blazing vertical wall of yellow/red fire spanning all frequencies (0.5 to 25+ Hz), with intense low-frequency resonance (1–5 Hz) that persists across the waterfall for tens of seconds.

💡 Observatory Guide — How the 24-Hour Helicorder Drum Works ▼ Details
ℹ️ WHAT IT MEASURES

Traditional seismological 24-hour rotating drum record. Each horizontal row is 1 full UTC hour (60 minutes left-to-right). The bottom row is the current hour with the red live stylus needle.

🟢 WHAT IS NORMAL (AMBIENT)

Nighttime quiet hours show clean, thin horizontal lines. Daytime human activity shows gentle thickening (1–2 mm). Hours before station startup remain clean blank paper.

⚡ WHAT AN EARTHQUAKE LOOKS LIKE

A dramatic, spindle-shaped packet with large vertical deflections that may cross multiple rows. Local quakes show sharp onset; distant regional quakes show wide, smooth rolling spindles with gold USGS magnitude pins.

24-Hour Helicorder Drum Record (EHZ)

Continuous 24-hour strip record (1 hour per row). Quakes appear as multi-minute packet bursts crossing rows.

💡 Observatory Guide — Understanding STA/LTA & RSAM Energy ▼ Details
ℹ️ WHAT IT MEASURES

STA/LTA: Ratio of Short-Term Average (1s) to Long-Term Average (10s) background. RSAM: Real-time Seismic Amplitude Measurement (continuous 1-minute velocity power).

🟢 WHAT IS NORMAL (AMBIENT)

Normal STA/LTA ratio fluctuates between 0.8x and 1.5x (trigger threshold is calibrated to 6.5x to prevent false alarms). Normal background RSAM is <50 counts. Heavy trucks briefly bump RSAM to 150–400 counts.

⚡ WHAT AN EARTHQUAKE LOOKS LIKE

STA/LTA spikes vertically past 6.5x (often 15x–100x) within 0.1s. RSAM surges into thousands (2,000 to 50,000+ counts) and remains elevated across consecutive 1-minute bars.

💡 Observatory Guide — Reading 3D Particle Motion Orbits ▼ Details
ℹ️ WHAT IT MEASURES

3D ground particle motion vector trajectory combining North-South, East-West, and Vertical strong-motion accelerometer axes.

🟢 WHAT IS NORMAL (AMBIENT)

Small random isotropic cluster or jitter centered at (0,0). Local footsteps appear as tiny vertical loops.

⚡ WHAT AN EARTHQUAKE LOOKS LIKE

P-Wave: Elongated linear needle pointing directly along the back-azimuth toward/away from the epicenter. Rayleigh Waves: Retrograde rolling elliptical loop in the vertical plane.

Tri-Axial Ground Motion Orbit (Hodogram)

Real-time particle trajectory combining ENN (N-S), ENE (E-W), and ENZ (Vertical) strong-motion accelerometer vectors.
